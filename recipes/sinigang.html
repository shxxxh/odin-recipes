Description

Sinigang is a classic Filipino soup and main dish defined by its distinct sour and savory broth. The word comes from the Tagalog root sigang, which means "to stew". It is widely considered a cornerstone of Filipino comfort food and is traditionally served hot alongside steamed white rice

Ingridients

  • Pork belly
  • garlic
  • Onion
  • souring Agents
  • Spinach
  • Egglplant
  • okra

Instructions

  1. Boil the pork (or main protein): Place your pork belly or ribs in a large pot with water. Bring to a rolling boil, then lower the heat, cover, and simmer for 45 to 60 minutes until the meat is tender. Skim off any gray foam (scum) that floats to the top during the first few minutes to keep the broth clean and clear.
  2. Add the aromatics: Toss in quartered onions and tomatoes. Simmer them with the pork for about 5 to 10 minutes so their flavors melt into the broth.
  3. Brown the chicken: Heat 1 tbsp of cooking oil in a pan, then sear the chicken pieces skin-side down until golden brown on both sides.
  4. Add the souring agent: Stir in your tamarind soup base mix, tamarind paste, or boiled/mashed fresh tamarind juice. Stir well until fully dissolved into the liquid..
  5. Simmer without stirring: Bring the liquid to a full boil, and do not stir for the first 5 minutes to let the raw vinegar cook off.
  6. Season with fish sauce: Add fish sauce (patis) to taste for that savory, salty foundation..
  7. Add the tender-crisp vegetables: Add eggplant, long green beans (sitaw), okra, and whole green chili peppers (siling pangsigang). Cook for another 3 to 5 minutes until just tender..
